---
title: "Familiar Faces: Pre-existing Teammate Ties and Post-Trade Production"
status: working-paper

When a player is traded in the middle of an NHL season, he must produce in a new system before he has learned it. We ask whether a pre-existing teammate already on the destination roster eases that move, and whether the benefit reaches the teammate who supplies it. Using 488 arrivals across eleven seasons and game-level expected goals, we follow both sides of each trade: the newcomer, compared before and after the move, and the incumbents who receive him, compared within the same team and game by whether they had played with him before. A newcomer who arrives with a former teammate creates about seven percent more scoring chances after the trade; the gain arrives with the first familiar face and does not grow in proportion to the count. The incumbents who knew him gain nothing, and the confidence interval rules out spillovers larger than about one and a half percent of average chance creation. The benefit of a tie falls to the player doing the adjusting, not to the team he joins. The pattern fits a tie that eases entry into an unfamiliar system rather than one that raises the joint output of a reunited pair.
