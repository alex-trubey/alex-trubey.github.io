Working paper · 2026

Endogenous Network Formation in Information Markets

Abstract

We study a two-sided information market in which suppliers choose content quality and consumers choose whom to follow: how intensely to subscribe to suppliers directly, and how intensely to follow peers who reshare. Quality is sustained by an audience premium: high-quality stories spread further, so low-cost suppliers invest. Platform verification tools (fact-check labels, source badges) make each share more diagnostic of quality and always raise equilibrium quality when the network is held fixed. Once consumers adjust their links, the same policy lowers it: at our calibrated economy, a dose that raises quality by 0.025 on the frozen network lowers it by 0.054 along the equilibrium path. Verification makes peer evidence more convincing, which also strengthens the option of acting on peer evidence without subscribing at all: past a critical dose, following becomes self-reinforcing, the subscription-heavy equilibrium disappears in a fold, and the market tips to a free-riding configuration in which the audience premium for quality is negligible. The reversal extends beyond verification: throttling peer transmission triggers the same collapse at matched strength, so quality-improving policies near the tipping point self-defeat as a class. Two further results characterize the endogenous architecture: supplier-link cost asymmetry generates a core-periphery peer layer with competing routing and capacity effects on quality, and quality peaks at intermediate connectedness even though connectedness is chosen, not fixed.

Working paper · 2024, updated 2026

Social Networks and Success in the YouTube Creator Economy

Abstract

This paper maps the collaboration network of English-language YouTube commentary creators and examines how a creator’s network position relates to their success. The dataset combines YouTube Data API records with 2,917 hand-verified collaborations among 1,391 creators, where a collaboration is a video featuring two or more creators each making a meaningful contribution. This definition is stricter than the subscription, comment, or hyperlink ties used in prior YouTube network studies. The network is sparse and highly disconnected, with a core-periphery-like structure and slight homophily by race and gender. Degree and weighted-degree centrality are positively associated with view and subscriber counts; betweenness and eigenvector centrality show weaker associations. Channel age and total video count are also associated with success, with video count showing the stronger association. Many high-success creators sit on the network periphery without observed collaborations, suggesting that collaboration is one path to success among several. Because the analysis is observational and cross-sectional, the results are descriptive associations rather than causal effects.

Working paper · 2026

Familiar Faces: Pre-existing Teammate Ties and Post-Trade Production

Abstract

When a player is traded in the middle of an NHL season, he must produce in a new system before he has learned it. We ask whether a pre-existing teammate already on the destination roster eases that move, and whether the benefit reaches the teammate who supplies it. Using 488 arrivals across eleven seasons and game-level expected goals, we follow both sides of each trade: the newcomer, compared before and after the move, and the incumbents who receive him, compared within the same team and game by whether they had played with him before. A newcomer who arrives with a former teammate creates about seven percent more scoring chances after the trade; the gain arrives with the first familiar face and does not grow in proportion to the count. The incumbents who knew him gain nothing, and the confidence interval rules out spillovers larger than about one and a half percent of average chance creation. The benefit of a tie falls to the player doing the adjusting, not to the team he joins. The pattern fits a tie that eases entry into an unfamiliar system rather than one that raises the joint output of a reunited pair.
